About Me

Not the hardest start in life, but not the easiest either. A congenital condition that sometimes people don’t understand.

Grew up in Sunday School, family followed folk religion—struggled with faith, now more open-minded. Nearly drowned once, had a few major surgeries.

Curious about everything—maybe all my travels come from curiosity. Love different cultures, landscapes, and ways of life.

Love art; beauty is part of life. Self-taught in photography, carried a Polaroid camera while traveling, once studied UI/UX on my own.

Love learning, especially new ways of thinking and living. Dream of traveling the world.

One personality test said I’m “Artistic + Social.” In short: a contradiction, and also a workaholic who loves efficiency. I want work to involve growth, creativity, and variety.

Default setting: introverted HSP. After socialization, I adapt well—but I still need solitude, even “hermit time.” Sometimes I even avoid messages. I don’t see solitude as bad, though sometimes I wish I could share joy with loved ones.

I Am a Traveler

I’ve always been searching for a place called “home.” Some say environment doesn’t matter—if your heart settles, anywhere can be home. Nonsense… Mencius’ mother moved three times, schools and neighborhoods matter. Let’s not forget national policies, nature, education, infrastructure, culture, salaries.

Is this stepping outside the comfort zone? Do I even want to settle down? Maybe when I’m 35, I’ll know.

I like to keep things flexible. My view on love is just right. If things fall apart, maybe it’s just another chance to start again.

For me, “home” is simply whatever fits in my suitcase. Everything else spreads from there, but at the core—it’s that one suitcase.

With friends, sometimes we drift apart; sometimes new friends come and I need to treasure them. I still cherish those who once laughed with me. Maybe I’ll write Friends in Memory.

Being a traveler is complicated. The ones you remember forget you; the ones you forgot still remember you. As your stories grow, shared resonance stays in the past. When we finally meet again, you want to tell everything—but unlike Dune, you can’t read each other’s memories. I once joked I needed a PPT to tell my story—now I hope podcasting can do the job. Maybe it’ll feel like meeting me all over again.

As for family—Asian culture, traditional roots, filial piety. I know I’m loved, and that’s enough.
